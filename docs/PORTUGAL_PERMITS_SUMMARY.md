# Portugal Permits: Hands-On Summary

The 35-word version of [PORTUGAL_PERMITS_HIL.md](PORTUGAL_PERMITS_HIL.md). Not legal advice; confirm each step with ANAC.

**Indoor development.** Build and test inside a fully enclosed building on your property. EU drone rules generally don't apply indoors. Ventilate, wear a respirator, keep flames away, have an extinguisher ready, and log every test.

**Operator registration.** Register as a UAS operator on ANAC's portal (reportedly uas.anac.pt). The camera alone probably requires it. Label the Flip with your operator ID and enter the ID in the Remote ID settings in DJI Fly.

**Pilot competency.** Pass the free online A1/A3 course and exam on ANAC's platform. Then take the A2 certificate to strengthen your application. Start a flight logbook now; ANAC will want evidence of experience.

**Insurance.** Get civil liability insurance even below the 900 g threshold. Confirm in writing that it covers a modified drone, a flammable payload, Specific-category operations, and fire or property damage to your land and neighbouring land.

**Airspace check.** Check your property on Portugal's official UAS geographic zones map. Airports, heliports, military areas or nature reserves nearby mean extra authorisations or no flying. Save the screenshots for your application.

**Image capture.** Portugal has historically required separate AAN authorisation, through e-AAN, for aerial filming. Confirm whether filming your own property still needs it. Until it's confirmed, avoid recording and never film neighbours or passers-by.

**ConOps document.** Write a short operations document covering the aircraft, the payload (UN1950 aerosol), the location, height and distance limits, the cordon, the fire procedures, lost-link behaviour, the crew and the checklists. This is the core of your application.

**Ask ANAC first.** Email ANAC, in Portuguese, describing the operation. Ask whether a PDRA fits or SORA is needed, what dangerous-goods requirements apply, and about pilot training, insurance, fees and processing times. Save the reply and quote it.

**SORA assessment.** Assess ground risk (a controlled private area gives the lowest bracket), air risk (low and away from airports), SAIL (aim for I–II) and the safety objectives. Declare dangerous goods and attach your indoor test logs.

**Submit and wait.** Send the application, ConOps, SORA, insurance, certificates and maps through ANAC's Specific-category route. Expect one to three months, plus questions. Keep developing indoors and record every change to the drone.

**Fly by conditions.** Follow the authorisation exactly. Before each flight, run the checklist, check the wind, check the cordon and brief the spotter. Painting walls elsewhere later needs a new authorisation and the owner's written consent.
