# Flying the Grafiti Drone Legally in Portugal: Step-by-Step Guide

**Goal:** fly a DJI Flip with a spray-can attachment **over your own property, for personal development**, legally.

> **Not legal advice.** This is a research-based walkthrough compiled in November 2026. Rules, portals and fees change. Every step marked **CHECK** must be confirmed with ANAC (Autoridade Nacional da Aviação Civil) before you rely on it.

---

## Why this needs a permit at all

The stock DJI Flip (249 g, class C0) can fly in the EU **Open category** with almost no paperwork. Adding a spray can breaks this in two ways:

1. **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/947, Art. 4.** Open-category drones must not carry **dangerous goods** or **drop any material**. Aerosol paint is dangerous goods (UN1950), and spraying probably counts as dropping material.
2. **Mass and class.** The modified drone weighs more than 250 g and loses its C0 status.

So **every outdoor flight with a can fitted is a Specific-category operation**. That means you need an **operational authorisation from ANAC**, based on a risk assessment (SORA) or a predefined risk assessment (PDRA).

**Your own property helps a lot.** A fenced, controlled area with nobody else present sits in the lowest ground-risk bracket. That makes the risk assessment much lighter.

---

## Step 0: Develop indoors first (no aviation permit)

EU drone rules apply to flights in airspace. Flights **inside a fully enclosed building** (a garage, barn or warehouse) are generally outside their scope. **CHECK** whether ANAC treats netted cages or open-sided structures the same way. Assume they don't.

**What you do:**
- Run all bench, tether and spray tests inside an enclosed building on your property.
- Ventilate (open doors, fan extraction) and wear a respirator. Keep **no flames or heaters** nearby, and have a CO₂ extinguisher within reach.
- Protect the walls and floor. Use cardboard or plywood targets.
- Log every test, because these logs become evidence for your SORA later.

This covers **Months 1–2 of the roadmap** with no permit. Start the paperwork below in parallel.

---

## Step 1: Register as a UAS operator (ANAC)

- **Where:** ANAC's UAS operator registration portal, reported as **uas.anac.pt**. **CHECK** the URL on anac.pt.
- **Who:** anyone operating a drone over 250 g, or one with a camera that isn't a toy. The Flip has a camera, so you probably need this even unmodified.
- **You get:** an **operator ID**, reportedly valid for 5 years. Display it on the drone with a label, and enter it into the Remote ID settings in the DJI Fly app.
- **Cost:** reported as free or low-cost. **CHECK** this.

**What you do:** register, print the label and stick it on the Flip's body (not on the attachment).

---

## Step 2: Remote pilot competency

- **A1/A3 certificate (baseline):** an online course and exam on ANAC's remote pilot training platform, reportedly **rp.anac.pt**. **CHECK** the URL and whether it's free.
- **A2 CofC (recommended):** adds a self-practical declaration and a theory exam. It isn't strictly required, but it strengthens your application.
- **Specific-category competency:** the authorisation itself states what training ANAC requires. Expect to show at least A1/A3 + A2, plus practical training on your own drone (logged flight hours).

**What you do:** pass A1/A3 this month, then A2, and start a flight logbook (date, duration, location, purpose, notes).

---

## Step 3: Insurance

- Portuguese rules (Decree-Law 58/2018 and Portaria 2/2021, per secondary sources) reportedly require **civil liability insurance above 900 g**. Your modified Flip is lighter than that, but **get insurance anyway**. ANAC will probably expect it for a Specific-category authorisation, and a flammable payload is exactly what insurance exists for.
- **CHECK with the insurer** that the policy covers:
  - a **modified** drone with an attached payload
  - **Specific-category** operations
  - fire and property damage on your own land and neighbouring land

**What you do:** get 2–3 quotes from Portuguese insurers that list drone policies (e.g. Lusitania, NacionalGest, Seguitex). Send them the operation description from Step 5.

---

## Step 4: Check the airspace over your property

- Look up your property in Portugal's official **UAS geographic zones** map. ANAC and the "Voa na Boa" resources point to the official maps. Look for airports, heliports, military areas, protected areas and other restricted zones.
- If your land is inside a restricted zone, you'll need an extra authorisation from whoever manages that zone. Flying may also be impossible.

**What you do:** screenshot the map showing your property's coordinates and save it for the application.

---

## Step 5: Camera and image capture (AAN)

Portugal has historically required separate authorisation from the **AAN (Autoridade Aeronáutica Nacional)** for capturing aerial images, through the **e-AAN** portal. Search results don't confirm whether this still applies in 2026.

**What you do:** **CHECK** with AAN or e-AAN whether filming your own property needs authorisation. If you're unsure, ask, or don't record video during test flights until it's cleared. Never film neighbours.

---

## Step 6: Write your ConOps (Concept of Operations)

This is the core document, and you write it yourself. Keep it short and concrete:

| Section | What to write |
|---|---|
| Operator | Name, operator ID, pilot certificates |
| Aircraft | DJI Flip, mass with attachment (weighed), attachment description, photos, RF trigger (band, power, fail-safe) |
| Payload | Aerosol paint can: brand, volume (e.g. 150 ml), UN1950 classification, quantity per flight (one can) |
| Location | Address, coordinates, map, **fenced/controlled ground area**, distance to nearest road, building and neighbour |
| Flight envelope | Max height (e.g. 10 m), max distance (visual line of sight, e.g. 30 m), max speed, wind limit, daylight only |
| Ground risk mitigations | Nobody inside the cordon except crew, optional tether, low altitude, low speed |
| Fire and hazmat procedures | Extinguisher on site, can stored away from heat, crash response (stop spraying, isolate the battery, wait, inspect) |
| Emergency procedures | Lost link (DJI return-to-home disabled or set to a safe height, trigger fail-safe releases the nozzle), flyaway, injury |
| Crew | You as pilot, plus an observer/spotter |
| Maintenance and logs | Pre- and post-flight checklist, flight log, test logs from Step 0 |

---

## Step 7: Risk assessment (SORA or PDRA)

- **First, ask ANAC whether any PDRA fits.** Most predefined scenarios assume **no dangerous goods**, so you'll probably end up doing a full SORA. **CHECK** this.
- **SORA, in short:**
  1. **Ground risk class (GRC):** a small drone over a controlled ground area with nobody else present gives the lowest GRC.
  2. **Air risk class (ARC):** low altitude over private land away from airports is usually ARC-a or ARC-b.
  3. **SAIL:** these together should give **SAIL I or II**, the easiest level.
  4. **Operational safety objectives (OSOs):** show how you meet each one at the required level. Most are covered by your ConOps, training, checklists and logs.
  5. **Containment:** show the drone can't easily leave the area. Tethering, geofencing or a low height limit all help.
- **Dangerous goods:** declare it on the application form (there's a yes/no field). Describe the quantity, packaging and fire mitigations.
- EASA publishes SORA guidance and templates. Use the current SORA version.

**What you do:** fill in the SORA step by step and attach your Step 0 test logs as evidence.

---

## Step 8: Contact ANAC before you apply (recommended)

Ask before writing 30 pages. Draft email in Portuguese:

```
Assunto: Pedido de esclarecimento — operação UAS categoria específica em propriedade privada

Exmos. Senhores,

Sou operador UAS registado (n.º de operador: ______) e pretendo realizar,
exclusivamente para fins pessoais e de desenvolvimento, voos com um DJI Flip
equipado com um acessório que acciona uma lata de tinta em aerossol (≈150 ml,
UN1950), sobre propriedade minha, vedada e sem terceiros presentes, a baixa
altitude (≤10 m) e em VLOS.

Gostaria de confirmar:
1. Se esta operação se enquadra na categoria específica e qual o procedimento
   recomendado (SORA ou PDRA aplicável).
2. Requisitos relativos ao transporte de mercadorias perigosas (aerossol).
3. Requisitos de competência do piloto e de seguro.
4. Taxas aplicáveis e prazo estimado de análise.
5. Se é necessária autorização da AAN para captação de imagens na minha
   propriedade.

Com os melhores cumprimentos,
______
```

**What you do:** send the email, save the reply, and quote it in your application.

---

## Step 9: Submit the application and wait

- Submit the operational authorisation application through ANAC's Specific-category channel. The form and submission route are on anac.pt, under drones › categoria específica. Include the ConOps, SORA, insurance certificate, pilot certificates, operator ID, map and photos.
- **Fees and processing time:** not confirmed. Plan for **1–3 months** and possibly a round of questions. **CHECK** with ANAC.
- Keep testing indoors while you wait.

---

## Step 10: Fly by the conditions

- Read the authorisation carefully. Its limits (height, area, crew, conditions) override everything above.
- Before every flight: run the pre-flight checklist, check the wind, check the cordon and confirm the spotter is ready.
- After every flight: update the flight log, inspect the attachment and record anything unusual.
- Keep the authorisation and insurance on site (on your phone is fine).
- **Painting someone else's wall** later will need a new or amended authorisation for that location, plus written permission from the owner.

## Suggested timeline

| Week | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Operator registration, A1/A3 exam, airspace map check |
| 2 | Insurance quotes, email to ANAC, AAN check |
| 3–4 | A2 CofC, start the ConOps, indoor tests and logs |
| 5–6 | SORA draft, finalise the ConOps |
| 7 | Submit the application |
| 8–16 | Wait for ANAC; continue indoor development |
