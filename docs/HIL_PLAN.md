# Human-in-the-Loop (HIL) Monthly Plan

The hands-on work for December 2026 to March 2027: printing, assembly, manual testing and human judgement calls. Each subtopic is about 30 words.

---

## Month 1: December 2026, "Bench"

**Purchases.** Order micro servos, a sub-GHz RF pair, 1S LiPos, a boost converter, an arcade button, mini spray cans, PLA/PETG and scales. Keep the total under €120. Log every receipt and part number.

**Print attachments.** Print two or three open-source can-trigger designs in PLA. Weigh each part, note print time, settings and failures, check licences, and photograph everything for the public build log.

**Bench actuation.** Clamp each can and trigger it with the servo twenty times. Record spray success, servo strain, nozzle alignment and mount flex. Reject any design that misses more than one actuation.

**Lift ceiling.** Fly the Flip tethered with added dummy weights in small steps. Record hover stability, motor warnings and flight time. Set the maximum safe payload with a clear margin before designing further.

---

## Month 2: January 2027, "Tether"

**RF and firmware.** Flash button-to-servo firmware on both boards. Verify range, latency and fail-safe release on the bench. Check the link doesn't disturb the Flip's 2.4/5.8 GHz control or video.

**Clunky button.** Build an ugly but reliable box with a big button, arm switch and LED. Test fifty presses for false triggers, debounce issues and battery life. Appearance doesn't matter this month.

**Tethered spray.** Mount the full assembly and hover tethered in an open field against cardboard. Trigger five sprays. Film each one and record drift, downwash effect, CG shift and stability.

**Down-select.** Compare designs on mass, reliability, spray quality and ease of mounting. A human chooses one field design and documents the reasons publicly. Archive the losers along with what was learned.

**Legal outreach.** Contact the aviation authority, an insurer and a mural owner. Explain the project plainly, collect requirements in writing, and get the wall-permission template reviewed. Nothing flies free without answers.

---

## Month 3: February 2027, "First Wall"

**Site prep.** Visit the authorised wall. Check surroundings, people traffic, wind exposure and take-off zone. Sign the permission form, plan the cordon, brief the spotter and stage the extinguisher and PPE.

**First mural flight.** Fly short, low, slow passes over a pre-marked target zone. Trigger single bursts only. A human aborts on any wobble, gust or person entering. Film everything for honest documentation.

**Remote v1 print.** Design and print a handheld shell around the RF electronics, with a trigger button, arm toggle and lanyard. Check fit, grip comfort and button travel. Photograph it for project optics.

**Mount v2.** Model our own licence-clean mount using lessons from the MVP. Print, weigh and bench test it twenty times, then compare it against the chosen open design on every logged metric.

---

## Month 4: March 2027, "Repeat"

**Repeatability flights.** Log ten or more authorised flights. Measure hit accuracy, nozzle clogging, flight time and wind limits. A human reviews every log and flags anomalies before the next flight is approved.

**Remote v2.** Iterate on the grip, add a battery indicator and link-status LED, and reprint. Have three people try it blind and record their comments on comfort, trigger feel and accidental-press risk.

**Beta kits.** Assemble three to five kits by hand from the BOM. Time each assembly step, write instructions, and have a non-builder assemble one kit unaided to test how clear the documentation is.

**Artist pilot.** Give beta kits to vetted artists who hold signed wall permissions. Supervise their first flights, collect structured feedback, and document their work, with credit, as the project's first showcase.

**Go/no-go review.** Review payload limits, legal findings, costs and artist feedback. A human decides whether to proceed toward the product, pivot to non-aerosol delivery or narrow the launch markets. Publish the decision.
