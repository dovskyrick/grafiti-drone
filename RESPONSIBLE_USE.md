# Responsible Use & Legal Framework

> **This is not legal advice.** It is our current reading of the rules, published so users can see where we stand. Laws differ by country, region and city, and they change. Check with your aviation authority, your local council and a lawyer before you fly a payload.

---

## The short version

1. **Get written permission from the wall owner.** No permission means no flight.
2. **Check your aviation rules.** With an attachment, your Flip is no longer a sub-250 g drone.
3. **Check whether your regulator treats an aerosol can as hazardous material.**
4. **Don't fly over people.** Cordon the area, use a spotter, and keep a fire extinguisher on site.
5. **Get insurance** that covers drone operations with a payload.

If you can't tick all five, don't fly.

---

## 1. Property and permission

- Painting a wall without the owner's consent is **criminal damage / vandalism** in practically every jurisdiction. Using a drone may add aviation offences on top.
- Public walls often need a **municipal or arts-council permit** as well as the owner's consent.
- Use the [wall-permission template](#wall-permission-template) below. Keep a signed copy with you during the flight.
- We will not help, promote or feature work painted without permission.

## 2. Aviation: the weight-class change

The DJI Flip is sold under the 250 g threshold. **Adding a mount, servo, battery and can takes it over 250 g.** In practice:

| Region | What changes (our reading) | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| **EU (EASA)** | The C0 class label no longer applies once the drone is modified. The aircraft is treated as privately built and over 250 g, which in the Open category generally means **A3**: far from people and **150 m from residential, commercial, industrial or recreational areas**. Most urban murals will need **Specific-category** authorisation. | Operator registration, remote pilot competency, national authority guidance, Specific-category / PDRA options |
| **UK (CAA)** | Similar logic. Over 250 g changes Operator ID / Flyer ID requirements and the operating subcategory. | CAA Drone and Model Aircraft Code, class-mark rules for modified drones |
| **USA (FAA)** | Over 250 g requires **registration** and **Remote ID**. Commercial work falls under **Part 107**, and **14 CFR §107.36 prohibits carrying hazardous materials** (see §3). | Part 107 vs. recreational (49 USC 44809), waivers/exemptions, local and state rules |
| **Elsewhere** | Assume rules exist until you've confirmed otherwise. | Your national civil aviation authority |

## 3. Hazardous materials

Aerosol spray paint is typically classed as a **flammable compressed gas** (e.g. UN1950, Class 2.1). Some regulators, notably the US FAA under Part 107, **prohibit carrying hazardous materials** on small drones unless you hold a specific exemption. **We think this is the biggest regulatory question for the product**, and we will publish our legal findings here. Options we're exploring include exemptions, non-aerosol paint delivery, and limiting the product to markets where carriage is permitted.

## 4. Operational safety

- Fly **tethered** for all first tests.
- **Never** fly over or towards uninvolved people. Cordon the area and post a spotter.
- Keep the drone, battery and can away from heat and open flame. Have a **CO₂ or powder extinguisher** on site.
- Check wind before flying. Overspray drifts.
- Wear PPE (respirator, eye protection), and protect surrounding surfaces and cars.
- The trigger firmware **fails safe**: if the RF link drops, the nozzle is released.

## 5. Privacy

The Flip has a camera. Filming a mural session can capture people, windows and number plates. Follow local data-protection law (e.g. GDPR) and ask before filming identifiable people.

## 6. Warranty, trademarks and licences

- Fitting third-party attachments may **void your DJI warranty**.
- This project is **not affiliated with DJI**. "For DJI Flip" describes compatibility only.
- MVP testing uses third-party open 3D models under their own licences (often non-commercial). Those designs are credited and **not sold**. Commercial kits will use our own designs.

## Code of conduct

By using these designs, you agree to:

- Paint only walls you have written permission to paint
- Follow the aviation and hazardous-materials rules where you fly
- Keep people and property safe, and stop if conditions change
- Credit the project and share what you learn, especially failures
- Never use this to deface, harass or intimidate

## Pre-flight checklist

- [ ] Signed wall permission on site
- [ ] Aviation registration / authorisation valid for this weight and location
- [ ] Insurance active
- [ ] Area cordoned, spotter briefed, no uninvolved people
- [ ] Extinguisher and PPE on site
- [ ] Wind within limits
- [ ] Mount secure, CG checked, props and guards clear of the can
- [ ] RF link tested; fail-safe release verified on the ground
- [ ] Trigger battery and drone battery charged
